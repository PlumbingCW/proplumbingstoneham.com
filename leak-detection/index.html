Emergency leak detection in Stoneham, MA is for the leak you cannot see. The signs are a water bill that jumped, a meter that keeps turning when every tap is off, a ceiling stain, a warm spot on the floor, or a basement wall that is damp for no clear reason.

We use a meter test and listening gear to narrow it down, thermal imaging and moisture meters to confirm it, and the smallest opening needed to fix it. This page covers how leaks are found, what each tool does, and what to do if water is already coming through.

Active leak or wet ceiling: what to do first

Water running through a ceiling, wall or floor is urgent because it spreads along framing and into insulation. Our 24/7 line handles these calls, and a dispatcher can talk you through the shutoff.

Do these things first.

  1. Shut off the main water valve. In most homes it is near the water meter in the basement.
  2. If water is near a light fixture, outlet or ceiling fan, turn the power off at the breaker for that area.
  3. Move furniture and valuables away, and put a bucket under any active drip.
  4. Take photos and a short video for your insurance claim before cleanup begins.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and describe where the water appears and when it started.

A high water bill or a meter that will not stop

The simplest leak test uses your own water meter. Turn off every faucet and appliance, then look at the meter's small leak indicator or its dial. If it keeps moving, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. If it holds still, the leak may be after a fixture, such as a running toilet, or the high bill may come from a billing or seasonal issue.

Stoneham Public Works says it replaces water meters on a 15-year cycle and reads them quarterly and monthly. If your meter is older than that or looks damaged, ask Public Works to check it before assuming a leak. If the meter is fine and still moving, call us.

Ceiling, wall and under-house leaks

Water travels, so the stain on a ceiling is often not under the leak. It can come from a supply line, a drain, a shower pan, or a roof or window problem that looks like plumbing. We start by testing the plumbing, since that is what we can confirm quickly, and we tell you if it points elsewhere.

In a house with a crawl space or an unfinished basement, a leak under the floor can run for weeks before it shows. Listening gear and a moisture meter locate it without tearing up flooring, and a repair is made from below when possible.

Buried lines, irrigation and the yard

A leak in the water service line between the street and the house shows up as a meter that moves with the house shut off, low pressure, or a soft spot in the lawn. If your system has an irrigation line, a stuck zone valve can also leak a steady flow. We can isolate the zone, test it, and locate the break by sound.

For pool or spa leaks, we handle the plumbing lines and fittings. A pool shell crack is a different trade, and we will say so if the test points there. See our water line repair page when the leak is confirmed on the service line.

Stoneham water, pipes and why leaks form here

Stoneham's water comes entirely from the MWRA, which describes its water as soft, about 16 mg/L or 1 grain per gallon. Soft water is gentle on fixtures, so leaks in Stoneham homes tend to come from age, freezing, or poor fittings more than scale.

Public housing data put the typical home here at around the late 1950s, and many are older. Pipes in unheated areas and exterior walls take freeze stress in winter. After a cold snap, a small pinhole or a failed joint can show up as a new stain, so it pays to check a basement ceiling and a garage wall in the weeks after a hard freeze.
