Business plumbing emergency: what to do first

A commercial emergency has two goals: stop the damage and protect people. If the problem affects customers or staff, close the affected area and call the 24/7 line.

Follow these steps.

  1. Shut off water to the affected area or the whole building at the main valve, and know where each is before an emergency.
  2. If sewage or grease is coming up, close the area to customers, and keep staff out of standing water.
  3. Turn off power to wet equipment and do not walk through water to reach a panel.
  4. Photograph the damage for your insurer, and note the time you first saw it.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher the building type, the fixtures affected and who can give access.

Grease traps and kitchen lines

Restaurants and cafes send fats, oils and grease into the drain every day. Grease cools and sticks to the pipe wall, and over time it narrows the line until it backs up. A grease trap or interceptor catches much of it, but only if it is cleaned on schedule.

A scheduled jetting visit on a set interval costs less than an emergency call on a Saturday night. We can inspect your line with a camera, tell you how much buildup is there, and suggest an interval. Local sewer rules for food service can vary, so check with the Stoneham Board of Health and Public Works for your location.

Backflow prevention and code items

Many commercial buildings need backflow preventers on water lines that serve irrigation, boilers, fire protection or other equipment. These devices protect the public water supply from contamination, and they must be tested on a schedule by a certified tester.

If you are not sure what your building needs, ask Stoneham Public Works or the Building Division. We can coordinate with a certified backflow tester when a device needs service or a test report, and we repair or replace a device that has failed.

Working around your business

We schedule non-emergency work for off hours when possible. If the problem is urgent, we focus on the fix first and plan the cleanup around your customers. You get a clear price before work starts, and a written report if you need one for a landlord or an insurer.

For landlords and property managers with several tenants, we can look at the building, mark shutoff valves and note which fixtures serve which units. That one page saves time on every future call.

  • Mark and label main and branch shutoff valves.
  • Keep a list of fixtures by tenant or area.
  • Know where cleanouts and floor drains are.
  • Keep the access contact on file with the dispatcher.
  • Schedule a camera look at older main lines before they fail.
