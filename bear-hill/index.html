Bear Hill is a residential area of Stoneham with Main Street on its west side. Its homes surround Bear Hill Golf Club, along wide, winding streets with mature trees. Joseph C. Cerrone Memorial Park and Rounds Playground are close, and the Middlesex Fells trails lead to Bear Hill Tower.

The name also belongs to the hill itself, which early colonists called Cheese Rock in 1632. This page is about the neighborhood around it, and like most neighborhoods it has no sharp boundary. Call and describe your street if you are not sure.

The homes around the golf club

Real estate listing data describes mostly single-family homes, with some townhouses and condos. The common styles are Colonial Revival and Cape Cod, with some larger newer houses. The data puts the median year built at 1959 and the typical lot at about 10,450 square feet, and many yards have fences.

Trees, yards and the lines underneath

Mature trees are part of what makes the area attractive. As a general plumbing fact, roots search for moisture and can work into joints in an older sewer line, in any neighborhood with large trees. That is a question to ask about your own line, not a claim about any one street.

If a drain keeps slowing down or the main backs up, a sewer camera inspection shows what is in the pipe. Cable work with sewer line cleaning clears a blockage, and hydro jetting is the tool for roots and heavy buildup that a cable leaves behind.

Outdoor plumbing and winter

Hose bibs, garage lines and pipes in exterior walls are the parts of a house most exposed to a New England winter. Disconnect hoses in the fall, shut off and drain outdoor lines where your house is set up for that, and open cabinet doors on exterior walls during a hard freeze.

If a pipe does freeze or split, shut off the water at the main and call. A burst pipe repair visit stops the water and replaces the damaged section. A leaking or low-pressure line between the street and the house is a job for water line repair.

Quick checklist for a Bear Hill house

  • Know where the main shutoff is and test it once a year.
  • Look at the water heater's date label and plan ahead.
  • Watch your water meter for movement when everything is off.
  • Keep the area around floor drains and the sump pit clear.
