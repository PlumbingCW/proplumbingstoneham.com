Emergency garbage disposal repair in Stoneham, MA is usually a jam, a leak, or a unit that has stopped working. A disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed. One that does nothing at all has lost power or tripped its reset. A wet cabinet is a leak, and the source can be the disposal itself or a drain connection nearby.

This page helps you tell which one you have, what is safe to try, and when the unit should be replaced. Never put your hand in a disposal, even when it is off.

Disposal leaking or backed up

A leak under the sink can ruin the cabinet floor, and a backed-up disposal can send gray water into the second sink. Our 24/7 line is open at all hours.

Start with these steps.

  1. Turn off the wall switch and unplug the unit or switch off its breaker before anything else.
  2. Stop using the sink and the dishwasher, because the dishwasher drains through the disposal in most kitchens.
  3. Empty the cabinet, dry it, and set a bucket under the leak to see where the water comes from.
  4. Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into the disposal.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and describe whether the water is coming from the top, the side or the bottom of the unit.

Which fault do you have?

Disposals fail in predictable ways. The symptom usually points to the cause, and most of them are fixable without replacing the unit.

What you noticeLikely causeWhat the plumber does
Hums, nothing spinsJam from a bone, peel or utensilFree the flywheel with a hex key, clear the item
Silent, no humTripped reset or no powerPress the red reset, test the outlet and switch
Leaks from the topFailed sink flange sealReseat the flange with new putty
Leaks from the side or bottomCracked housing or a worn sealReplace the unit
Slow drain, gurglingClog in the trap or the drain lineClean the trap, cable the line

Freeing a jam safely

Turn off power first. Most disposals have a socket at the bottom for a hex key that turns the flywheel by hand. Working the key back and forth frees the jam. Pull out the item with tongs or pliers, never a hand, and then press the reset button on the bottom of the unit.

If the unit is not free or trips again right away, the motor may be damaged, and that is when repair is not worthwhile.

Leaks: flange, drain pipe or the housing

A leak is usually at one of three places. The sink flange, where the disposal meets the sink, seals with putty. The drain outlet and dishwasher hose connections use gaskets and clamps. The housing itself corrodes after years of use.

We find the source by running water and watching. A flange or a gasket is a repair. A leaking housing is not repairable and the unit should be replaced. See our drain cleaning page if the leak is paired with a clog.

Replacing the disposal

Most disposals last around 8 to 15 years. If yours is older and has a bad motor or a leaking housing, a new unit costs less than the labor for another repair. Horsepower from one-third to one horsepower covers most homes, and a larger motor handles tougher scraps and runs quieter.

Installation includes the new mount, electrical connection, dishwasher knockout if needed, and a leak test. If your sink has no outlet under it, a plumber can coordinate with an electrician for a switched outlet.

What belongs in a disposal

A disposal does not handle everything, and the drain line behind it can still clog. A grease-lined kitchen line is a case for hydro jetting.

  • Run cold water before, during and for 15 seconds after grinding.
  • Feed scraps in small amounts, not in a rush.
  • Keep grease, oil, coffee grounds, eggshells in quantity, pasta and rice out of it.
  • Never grind fibrous peels like celery or corn husks in volume.
  • Never put non-food items, utensils or bottle caps in, and keep hands out.
