Emergency burst pipe repair in Stoneham, MA is a race against water. A burst or split pipe can release gallons a minute, and the longer it runs, the more damage spreads into ceilings, floors and insulation. The first job is to stop the water, and the second is to repair the pipe correctly.

In New England winters, most burst pipes are frozen pipes that split when ice expanded inside. The rest come from corroded pipes, high pressure, or a failed fitting. This page covers what to do right now and how repairs are made.

A pipe has burst: steps for the next ten minutes

Call the 24/7 line as soon as the water is shut off. We dispatch around the clock, and a dispatcher can stay on the phone while you work through the steps.

Here is what to do before the plumber arrives.

  1. Turn off the main water valve, usually near where the water line enters the house or next to the meter.
  2. Open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain the remaining water, and flush toilets once to empty tanks.
  3. If water has reached outlets, lights or appliances, shut off power at the breaker panel, and do not walk through standing water to reach it.
  4. Move belongings out of the wet area and use towels and buckets to limit spread.
  5. Photograph the damage and the pipe for your insurance claim, then call the 24/7 line.

Why pipes burst in Stoneham

Water expands as it freezes, and the pressure builds in the pipe between the ice blockage and a closed faucet. The split often shows up at the ice plug or at a weak point. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated basements, crawl spaces, garages and attics are the usual victims.

The break may not leak until the ice thaws, which is why a ceiling can look dry in the morning and soak through in the afternoon. After a hard freeze, check pipes that were exposed to cold, and look for frost on them. If the faucets run with no flow, a pipe may be frozen.

Frozen but not burst: what to do

If a pipe is frozen and has not split, open the faucet, and warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer or heating pad on its lowest setting, starting at the faucet end and working toward the frozen area. Never use a torch or open flame, because it can ignite framing or burst the pipe from the heat.

If you cannot find the frozen section, or if the pipe is inside a wall, call us. Our crew can thaw a line safely and check for splits before the ice melts and water starts to flow.

How burst pipes get repaired

The repair depends on the pipe. A split in copper is cut out and replaced with a new section and fittings. A cracked PEX line is cut and spliced with approved fittings. A corroded galvanized or older steel pipe may need more than a spot repair, and a few failures in a short time point to repiping.

Once the pipe is fixed, the plumber restores water slowly and watches for new leaks, since a freeze can weaken more than one spot. We also look at the cause. A pipe that froze in an exterior wall may need insulation or a relocated section so it does not freeze again.

After the repair: water damage and drying

Burst pipes often leave wet drywall, insulation and flooring. We stop the leak and fix the pipe, and we can tell you what we see. Drying and mold prevention need to start quickly, usually within a day or two, so call your insurer early and ask about a water mitigation company if the wet area is large.

Keep receipts and photos of everything, from the pipe itself to the damaged materials. Do not throw out damaged items until the adjuster has seen them unless they pose a health risk.

Preventing the next freeze in a Stoneham home

A frost-free hose bib is worth installing if your outdoor spigot has frozen before. Our faucet repair team can replace one in a short visit.

  • Insulate pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls.
  • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor spigots from inside before the first hard freeze.
  • Keep the heat on at 55 degrees or higher if you travel in winter.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls on the coldest nights.
  • Know where your main shutoff is, and test it once a year so it turns when you need it.
