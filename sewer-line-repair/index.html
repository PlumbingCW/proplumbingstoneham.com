Emergency sewer line repair in Stoneham, MA begins with finding out what is wrong, and that means a camera. A sewer line that backs up, smells, or leaves a soggy patch in the yard has failed in one of a few ways. Roots have entered a joint, the pipe has cracked, a section has sagged, or a section has collapsed.

We run a camera down the line, show you the video, and quote the repair that fits. Sometimes that is a localized spot repair, sometimes lining, and sometimes replacement of a longer stretch. This page lays out the options and how to choose.

A sewer line that has failed: what to do

Sewage in the basement or a lawn that has gone wet and foul is an urgent problem, and our line is staffed 24/7. If the pipe has collapsed, a cable will not solve it, so the first visit is usually a camera and a plan.

Do these things while you wait.

  1. Stop water use in the house, because every flush and shower adds to the backup.
  2. Keep family and pets away from standing wastewater and away from any wet area near electrical outlets.
  3. Take photos of any water damage for your insurer before anything is moved or cleaned.
  4. If the smell is strong, ventilate, and leave the house if anyone feels ill.
  5. Call the 24/7 line. Tell the dispatcher if there is a known cleanout, and if the yard has wet spots.

Spot repair, lining or replacement

The camera tells us whether the damage is local or spread along the pipe. One broken joint close to the house is a spot repair, where a short section is dug up and replaced. A pipe with several small defects over a long run may suit lining, where a resin-saturated liner is cured inside the old pipe. A pipe that has collapsed or lost its slope needs a section of new pipe.

Roots: a common reason older lines fail

Tree roots look for moisture and find it at the joints of older clay and cast iron lines. Once inside, they catch paper and grease, and the line clogs again and again. Cutting the roots with a jetter or a cable clears the pipe for a while, and a repair or liner closes the entry so they do not return.

A mature tree near the sewer line can be the right call to leave alone once the pipe is sealed. We will not recommend removing a tree unless the pipe cannot be saved any other way.

Permits and who does the street work

Stoneham's Building Division enforces the plumbing and gas codes, and it accepts applications online. Rules for a given repair vary, so ask the Building Division at Town Hall whether your project needs a permit and an inspection. A repair that stays within your property is handled between you, your plumber and the Building Division.

If the failure is in the street main, the work belongs to the town. Stoneham Public Works operates about 75 miles of sewer main, and its Water and Sewer page lists responding to sewer plugs and odor complaints. Your plumber can mark the camera footage at the property line so you know which side the break is on.

What happens on the day of a repair

  1. The camera run is repeated to confirm the location and depth.
  2. The area is marked, and utilities are located before any digging begins.
  3. The section is repaired or lined, then a second camera pass confirms the pipe is sound.
  4. The trench is backfilled and the yard is left level for settling. A lining job needs no trench.
  5. You get the before and after video and a written summary of the work.

Buying a house with an older sewer

If you are buying a home built decades ago, a camera inspection before closing is cheap compared with a surprise lateral replacement. We can run the camera, record the footage, and mark any defects so your agent and attorney can use the report.

The same video helps a seller who wants to disclose the condition of the line honestly and price the house accordingly.

What affects the price of sewer line repair

Length and depth

A short, shallow section is a small dig. A long or deep run takes longer and costs more.

Repair method

Spot repair, lining and replacement have different labor and material costs, and the camera picks the one that fits.

Surface above the pipe

Lawn is easier to restore than a driveway, a patio, or a slab.

Access and utilities

Tight side yards and nearby utilities slow the work.

Permits

If your repair needs a permit, the cost and timing are part of the quote.

Homeowner insurance usually does not cover a worn-out sewer line itself, though some policies cover resulting damage or offer a service-line endorsement. Ask your agent before work starts.

Sewer and drain repairs fall under 248 CMR 10.00, the Uniform State Plumbing Code, and many repairs need a local permit.
