Trees, roots and sewer laterals

Reading has a lot of mature trees, and trees and sewer pipes do not get along. Roots find the joints in older clay and cast iron laterals and grow inside them. The result is a drain that clogs, clears and clogs again.

A sewer line cleaning with a cutting head clears the roots. A camera inspection shows how many entry points there are. If the pipe has several, a repair or a liner closes them so the roots do not return.

  • A drain that clogs every few months.
  • Gurgling in the toilet when the washer drains.
  • A green or soggy patch in the yard over the sewer line.
  • Slow drains on every floor.
  • Sewage smell at the basement floor drain.

Housing around the Common and the Ipswich River

Reading's town center and the streets around the Common have older homes, and the town sits near the headwaters of the Ipswich River. Older homes carry older plumbing, and areas close to the river and its wetlands can have higher groundwater in wet months.

A basement in that setting benefits from a working sump pump. A slow drain on the main floor calls for drain cleaning, and a history of backups calls for a camera before more money is spent.
