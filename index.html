Plumbing emergency in Stoneham? Do these three things first

Water damage grows by the minute, so the first ten minutes matter more than anything else. You do not need to diagnose the problem to make it safer, and a dispatcher can talk you through each step while you hold the line.

Then call, tell us what you see and where the water is, and we send the nearest available plumber.

  1. Shut off the main water valve, usually near the meter in the basement. For a water heater leak, close the cold valve above the tank and turn off its power or gas.
  2. Turn off electricity near any wet area if you can reach the panel without standing in water, and keep children and pets away from sewage.
  3. Photograph the damage for your insurer, then call the line and describe what you see.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside. For a carbon monoxide alarm, get everyone out and call 911.

Stoneham plumbing: what the town's age and water mean for your pipes

Stoneham is a Middlesex County town of roughly 24,000 people, centered on Stoneham Center and wrapped around Spot Pond and the Middlesex Fells Reservation. Public housing data put the typical home at around the late 1950s, and many are older. That matters because older homes tend to have cast iron or clay drain lines, and galvanized or old copper supply pipes, which are the materials behind most slow drains and pinhole leaks.

The town's water comes from the MWRA system and is described as soft, at about 1 grain per gallon, so scale is rarely the issue here. Age is. Stoneham Public Works runs the town's water and sewer systems, including about 75 miles of sewer main, and it handles sewer plug and odor calls in the street. If our camera shows a problem past your property line, we mark it so you can show Public Works exactly where to look.

We reach most Stoneham addresses by Route 28 and Main Street, and we reach Woburn, Wakefield, Melrose and Winchester by Interstate 93 and Route 128. Near Bear Hill, Stone Zoo or the Stoneham Center business district, tell the dispatcher which, and we will find you faster.
