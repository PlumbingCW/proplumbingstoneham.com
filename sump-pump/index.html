Basement taking on water: first steps

Standing water in a basement is both a property risk and a safety issue, because of electrical outlets and appliances. Our line is open 24/7, including during storms.

Here is what to do.

  1. Stay out of the water if it is near outlets, the panel or appliances, and shut off power at the breaker only if you can reach the panel dry.
  2. Check that the pump is plugged in, the breaker has not tripped, and the float moves freely in the pit.
  3. Pour a bucket of water into the pit to see if the pump starts.
  4. Move valuables up, and use buckets or a wet vacuum to limit spread if it is safe.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher whether the pump runs, hums or is silent.

Why sump pumps fail

A sump pump sits in a pit that collects groundwater from drain tile or from under the slab. When the water rises, a float switch turns the pump on and it sends water out through a pipe to the yard or a storm drain. Each part can fail.

SymptomLikely causeFix
Pump will not startNo power, tripped breaker, stuck float or failed switchCheck power, free the float, replace the switch or pump
Hums but does not pumpClogged impeller or a seized motorClear the intake, replace the pump
Runs constantlyStuck float, failed check valve or very high groundwaterReposition the float, replace the check valve
Runs but does not move waterClogged discharge line, frozen pipe, or a broken check valveClear and thaw the line, replace the valve
Loud noise or vibrationDebris in the pit, worn bearingsClean the pit, replace the pump
Short cyclingPit too small or float set poorlyAdjust, or install a larger pit

Replacement and installation

Most pumps last around 7 to 10 years. If yours is older and has started acting up, replace it before the storm that breaks it. A new pump, a new check valve and a fresh float are inexpensive compared with water damage.

A new installation starts with the pit. If you have no pit, a hole is cut in the slab, a basin is set, and the discharge line is run to the outside. The discharge must send water far enough from the foundation that it does not just run back toward it.

Battery backup and water-powered backup

A sump pump needs power, and storms often cause outages. A battery backup pump runs on a deep-cycle battery and takes over when the main pump fails or the power is off. The battery lasts hours, not days, and needs replacement every few years.

Some homes with municipal water can use a water-powered backup, which runs on water pressure and needs no electricity. It uses a lot of water while running, and it must be allowed by the local water rules. Ask us which type fits your home and your discharge setup.

  • Test the main pump every few months by pouring water into the pit.
  • Test the battery backup at the same time.
  • Clean the pit of debris once a year.
  • Check the discharge outlet outside for blockage and freezing.
  • Replace the check valve if it clatters when the pump stops.

Where the discharge should go

Stoneham's Public Works department says it works to reduce stormwater and groundwater getting into the sewer system. For homeowners, that means a sump pump should not be connected to the sanitary sewer in most cases.

Rules for sump discharge differ by town, so ask the Stoneham Building Division or Public Works what is allowed at your address. We install the discharge line to the outside, away from the foundation and neighbors, and tell you if the layout needs a different approach.

A pump is one part of a dry basement

Pumps handle water that reaches the pit. They do not stop water from entering in the first place. If your basement takes in water often, or has stains, efflorescence or damp walls, a pump is a patch and not a cure. See our basement waterproofing page for interior drains, wall sealing and grading.

Public housing data put the typical Stoneham home at around the late 1950s. Houses of that era often have older foundations with some cracks and a basement floor that is less tight than a modern slab. We look at how water gets in, not only how it gets out.

What affects the price of sump pump

New pump or repair

A replacement of the pump alone is quick. A new pit and discharge line take more work.

Pump type

A higher horsepower pump or a battery backup system adds cost.

Discharge route

A short run through a rim joist is easy. A long run or one through a finished wall costs more.

Slab cutting

A new pit means cutting concrete, which adds time and dust control.
