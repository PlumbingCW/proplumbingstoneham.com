Tank failed: what to do before the plumber arrives

A failed tank can release its whole contents, so act quickly. Our 24/7 line is open at all hours, and replacements are often possible the next business day or sooner.

Here is what to do while you wait.

  1. Shut the cold water valve above the tank and switch off its breaker or turn the gas control to off.
  2. Hook a hose to the drain valve and run it to a floor drain, or let the water drain to a bucket in stages.
  3. Photograph the damage, the tank and the data plate for your insurer.
  4. Move anything that can be damaged out of the area, and mop up water to prevent mold.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher the tank size and whether it is gas or electric.

Sizing the new heater

The right size depends on how many people use hot water and how much at once. A tank that is too small runs out in the middle of a shower, and one that is too large wastes energy keeping extra water hot. The first-hour rating on the yellow energy label is the number that matters, more than tank size alone.

As a rough guide, a two-person household is often served by a 40-gallon tank, and a family of four commonly needs 50 to 75 gallons. A plumber will confirm by asking about bathrooms, soaker tubs and appliances.

What gets replaced and what gets upgraded

A proper replacement is more than setting a new tank in place. The plumber replaces the supply connectors and shutoff valves if they are worn, checks the relief valve and its discharge pipe, and installs a drain pan under the unit if the location calls for one.

If the house has a pressure reducing valve or a check valve at the meter, an expansion tank is needed so the extra pressure from heated water has somewhere to go. It is a small part that protects the new heater and the faucets.

  • New flexible connectors and a working shutoff valve
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve and discharge pipe
  • Expansion tank where the plumbing is a closed system
  • Venting check and correction on gas units
  • Drain pan with a drain line where the unit sits above finished space
  • Old tank removed and recycled

Permits, codes and who may do the work

In Massachusetts, plumbing work and gas fitting are regulated under 248 CMR, and the work is performed by licensed plumbers and gas fitters. The Stoneham Building Division issues the permits and does the inspections, and rules for a given job can differ, so ask the Building Division at Town Hall which permit applies to a heater swap.

We handle the permit steps as part of the visit when one is needed. You should not have to chase the paperwork.

Gas, electric and higher-efficiency options

Replacing like with like is the fastest path, since the existing gas line, vent or electrical circuit is already in place. Switching fuel or moving to a power-vent or condensing model can lower fuel use but may need new venting or electrical work, which adds time and cost.

A heat pump water heater is another option. It uses less electricity, needs space around it and some clearance for air, and cools the room it sits in a little. Ask us when you call if your layout might suit one. If you are weighing a tankless unit, our tankless water heater page covers when it pays off.

Installation day in a Stoneham home

Public housing data put the typical Stoneham home at around the late 1950s, and many basements in houses of that age have low ceilings and tight utility corners. If yours does, mention it when you call so the right size and vent type are on the truck.

  1. The plumber shuts off the water and gas or power, then drains the old tank.
  2. The old unit is disconnected and carried out. In many Stoneham basements this means a narrow staircase, so we plan the route first.
  3. The new tank is set, connected and vented, with new fittings, relief valve and expansion tank where needed.
  4. The system is filled, purged of air and checked for leaks. Gas connections are tested.
  5. You get a walkthrough of the shutoff valves, the temperature setting and the label with the warranty information.

What affects the price of water heater replacement

Tank size and type

A 40-gallon standard tank costs less than a 75-gallon high-recovery or a power-vent model.

Fuel and venting

Switching fuel or changing the vent adds work compared with a like-for-like swap.

Old unit condition

Corroded fittings and old shutoff valves are replaced during the job.

Code items

Expansion tank, relief piping and pan are added when the layout calls for them.

Timing
