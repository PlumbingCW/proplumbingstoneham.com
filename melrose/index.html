Emergency plumber service in Melrose, MA is organized here by the seasons, since the calls follow the weather. Winter brings frozen and burst pipes. Spring brings wet basements and failed sump pumps. Summer brings sewer backups after storms and water heater failures that went unnoticed. Fall is the time to prevent them.

Melrose and Stoneham share a border near the Middlesex Fells, and that puts a plumber close to the east side of the area. This page walks through the year and ends with how to prepare.

Winter: frozen pipes and burst pipes

Melrose homes with pipes in exterior walls, garages and unheated basements take the worst of a cold snap. A frozen pipe can split without leaking until the ice melts, and the leak shows up as a wet ceiling or a hissing wall.

If you suspect a frozen pipe, open the faucet, warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer and call us. If water is already running, shut off the main valve. Our burst pipe repair page has the full step list. Also see water line repair if the whole house loses water.

Spring: groundwater, sump pumps and wet basements

Snowmelt and spring rain raise groundwater, and basements that were dry in winter can take in water. A sump pump that has sat idle for months may stick, so test it by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. If it does not start, call.

Melrose is a city with many older homes on small lots, so water often has no place to go except toward the foundation. Gutters and downspouts that discharge several feet away make a real difference. For lasting solutions, see sump pump service and basement waterproofing.

  • Test the sump pump and the battery backup.
  • Clear gutters and extend downspouts.
  • Check the basement walls for new cracks or damp spots.
  • Know where your main shutoff is.
  • Move stored items off the floor in a basement that has taken water before.

Summer: heavy storms and sewer backups

Summer thunderstorms can drop a lot of rain quickly, and a sewer line that is already stressed may back up. A backup in a basement floor drain after a storm is a sign the lateral is partly blocked or taking in water. A sewer line cleaning visit restores flow, and a camera shows why it happened.

Hot weather is also when a leaking water heater goes unnoticed in a closet or a basement corner. If your tank is more than ten years old, look at the base for rust or moisture. See water heater repair for the checks.

Fall: get ahead of winter

Fall is the time to disconnect hoses, shut off outdoor spigots from the inside, insulate exposed pipes and have the boiler looked at. A boiler that fails in the first cold week is a common call, and our boiler repair page covers what we can do on the water side.

A short list of tasks done in October saves a long night in January.
