A sewer inspection in Stoneham, MA is a video look inside your main line using a waterproof camera on a flexible cable. It shows roots, cracks, offsets, sags, grease buildup and the pipe material, so the next decision is based on a picture and not a guess.

People book a camera visit before buying an older house, after a repeat clog, or before spending money on a repair. The visit is planned work, so it carries a standard title and not an emergency one, though our 24/7 line is open if the camera finds a problem that cannot wait.

What the camera shows

The camera head has lights and a transmitter, and the plumber watches a monitor as it moves down the line. A locator above ground picks up the signal, so the plumber can mark the depth and position of any defect on your lawn or driveway.

You get the video and a short written summary. The summary names the pipe material, lists each defect with its distance from the cleanout, and says whether the line needs cleaning, repair or nothing at all.

  • Roots entering joints or cracks
  • Cracked, broken or collapsed sections
  • Offset joints and bellies where water pools
  • Grease or scale buildup narrowing the pipe
  • Pipe material: cast iron, clay, plastic or fiber
  • Connections, cleanouts and any tie-ins from other buildings

Inspecting before you buy a house

A pre-purchase sewer inspection costs a small fraction of what a failed lateral costs to replace. Ask the seller for access to the cleanout. If the house has none, the plumber can sometimes enter through a toilet flange or a roof vent, though a cleanout gives a better picture.

If the camera finds a defect, your attorney and agent can use the video to negotiate a repair or a credit. If it finds a clean line, you buy with fewer unknowns. We can give the footage to you directly so you can share it with your inspector.

Repeat clogs and mystery smells

A drain that clogs every few months should be looked at, not just cleared again. The camera finds the cause, such as a root intrusion at a joint, or a pipe that has sagged and holds water.

A smell of sewage near a cleanout or in the yard is another reason for a camera visit. It may be a dry trap, a loose cap, or a cracked pipe, and the camera can sort those out quickly.

Stoneham housing and what the camera tends to find

Public housing data put the typical Stoneham home at around the late 1950s, with many homes older, so older materials and aging joints are common here. Cast iron and clay lines of that era can corrode and crack, and roots find those weak points.

Stoneham's Public Works department operates about 75 miles of sewer main, and the camera helps you see whether a defect sits on your lateral or out in the street. If the problem is in the town main, the footage with a depth mark gives Public Works what it needs to follow up.

What happens after the inspection

If the line is clear, you keep the video as a baseline for the future. If it needs cleaning, we can move to sewer line cleaning or hydro jetting when you are ready. If it needs a repair, we explain the options on our sewer line repair page and quote the one that fits your pipe.

Nothing is done without your agreement, and you get the price before work starts.

What affects the price of sewer inspection

Cleanout access

A cleanout makes the inspection faster. If none exists, access may take extra time.

Line length

A longer run from house to street takes more camera time.

Cleaning needed first

A line full of grease or debris may need cleaning so the camera can see the pipe.

Report and video

A written summary with marked defects and video takes some time and is included in the visit.

Scheduling

Planned visits are scheduled in advance, and urgent ones can be fit in on the 24/7 line.

Insurance does not usually pay for an inspection. A video report is useful when you file a claim for a backup, so keep a copy.

A camera inspection is a diagnostic step. Any repair it leads to is regulated under 248 CMR, the Massachusetts plumbing code.
