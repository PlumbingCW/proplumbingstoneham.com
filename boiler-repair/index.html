Emergency boiler repair in Stoneham, MA is a seasonal call, and it is one that cannot wait. A boiler that shuts off in January leaves a house to cool quickly, and a leaking or over-pressured boiler can be unsafe. We handle the water side of hot-water and steam systems, such as leaks, circulator pumps, zone valves, expansion tanks and pressure, and the gas piping and shutoff side.

Boiler work in Massachusetts involves more than one license, depending on the fuel and the part, so this page also says what we do and when a different technician is the right call.

No heat or a leaking boiler: first steps

Before you call, a few checks tell the dispatcher what you have. Our line is staffed 24/7 during the heating season.

Do these in order.

  1. Check the thermostat is set to heat, and has fresh batteries if it uses them.
  2. Check the boiler's power switch and the circuit breaker.
  3. Look at the pressure gauge. Cold, a hot-water boiler usually sits around 12 to 15 psi. Zero or above 30 means a problem.
  4. If you smell gas, leave the house, and call the gas utility from outside. If an alarm sounds, call 911.
  5. If water is leaking, turn off the boiler and the water supply to it, and call the 24/7 line.

Water side, gas side and who does what

Massachusetts divides plumbing, gas fitting and oil burner work by license. A plumber can repair the piping, valves, expansion tank, circulator and fill assembly on the water side. A licensed gas fitter handles gas piping and connections. Oil burner service uses a separate license for the burner and its controls.

When you call, tell the dispatcher the fuel and what the boiler is doing. We tell you plainly whether we can do the whole repair or whether a heating technician is the right visit for the burner.

Leaks, pressure and expansion tanks

A boiler is a closed system that holds water at a set pressure. The expansion tank gives the heated water room to expand, and the relief valve opens if pressure gets too high. A waterlogged expansion tank is a very common cause of a relief valve that drips.

A drip from the relief valve is a safety signal, and the valve should not be capped. It has to be repaired by replacing the part that caused it. Leaks from the boiler body itself usually mean the heat exchanger has failed, and a replacement is the answer.

Maintenance that prevents winter failures

Most no-heat calls in the first cold week of winter come from boilers that were not looked at since last year. A fall tune-up costs less than a January emergency.

  • Have the boiler serviced annually before the heating season.
  • Check the pressure gauge monthly in winter.
  • Keep the area around the boiler clear for air and access.
  • Bleed radiators or baseboards that feel cool at the top.
  • Know where the boiler's power switch and water shutoff are.

Older Stoneham homes and heating systems

Public housing data put the typical Stoneham home at around the late 1950s. Many houses of that era and earlier have hot-water baseboard or radiators fed by a boiler, and some have steam. Steam systems need different controls and care than hot water, so tell us which you have.

If your boiler is more than 20 years old and needs a major repair, ask whether a new unit is better. We can talk through the sizing and the options on the plumbing side, and a heating technician can address the burner and controls. For hot water from an indirect tank, see water heater repair.
