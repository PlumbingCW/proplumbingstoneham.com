Emergency water line repair in Stoneham, MA is for the pipe that brings water from the street to your house. A leak in it can show up as a wet lawn, a meter that spins with everything off, a sudden drop in pressure, or no water at all after a hard freeze.

This page explains how the line is tested, what repair methods exist, and who is responsible for which section. It also covers what a homeowner should check before calling. If water is gushing in the yard, jump to the urgent steps.

Water line broken or no water at all

A broken service line can release a lot of water, and the line may be underground, so the first step is to shut the water off. Our 24/7 line takes these calls at any hour.

Do the following.

  1. Shut off the main valve inside the house, usually near the meter. If it does not turn, call us right away.
  2. Turn off the water heater to avoid dry-firing the tank if the supply is lost, and shut off its power or gas.
  3. Look for a wet or sunken spot in the yard, and note where it is. Do not dig it yourself.
  4. If the water is near electrical equipment or a gas meter, keep away and call.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher what you see and where the meter is.

Signs of a service line problem

Any of these can have other causes, so the plumber starts with a meter test and a pressure check before digging. Our leak detection page explains how a hidden leak is located by sound.

  • The water meter keeps turning when every fixture is off.
  • A soft, wet or unusually green patch of lawn between the street and the house.
  • Pressure drops at every tap at once, not one fixture.
  • Sound of running water near the meter or the entry point.
  • A rising water bill with no change in use.
  • Discolored or muddy water after a pressure change.

Who owns which part of the line

In general, the town's water main and the connection to it belong to the water utility, and the pipe from the curb or the meter to your house belongs to you. The exact dividing point varies by address and by municipality. In Stoneham, the Public Works department runs the water system and is the right office to ask where the town's responsibility ends.

Stoneham buys its water from the MWRA, which supplies the town's system, and Public Works reads and replaces the water meters. If your problem is at or before the meter, call Public Works. If it is after the meter, call us. When in doubt, we can locate the leak and mark it so you know which side it is on.

Repair, trenchless and replacement

A small leak at a fitting or a short broken section is a spot repair, where the plumber digs to the pipe, cuts out the bad section and joins new pipe with approved fittings. A long section of old pipe, such as lead, galvanized steel or brittle copper, often makes more sense to replace than to patch.

Trenchless methods such as pipe pulling can replace a line with less digging. Whether it is possible depends on the soil, the depth and the route. A short list of questions answers that on the site visit.

Lead, old materials and replacement

Homes built decades ago can have service lines made of lead, galvanized steel, copper or an older plastic. Federal rules have pushed utilities to identify lead service lines. If you are not sure what your line is made of, look at where it enters the house and scratch the pipe gently. Lead is soft and dull gray.

If we find a lead line during a repair, we tell you. The path forward depends on the town's program and the length of line you own, so ask Public Works and your insurer before replacement is planned.

Frozen water lines in winter

Freezing is rare for lines buried below frost depth but common for lines that enter a house through a cold crawl space, an unheated garage or a shallow trench. If you lose water in a hard freeze, shut off the main and open a faucet, then call us.

We thaw a line safely and check for splits as the ice melts. See also burst pipe repair if a pipe inside the house has split.

What affects the price of water line repair

Depth and length

A shallow, short section is a small dig. A long or deep run takes more time.

Surface above the line

Lawn is easier to restore than a driveway, a walk or a patio.

Pipe material

Copper, plastic and lead replacements differ in cost and rules.

Access and utilities

Nearby gas, electric and other lines slow the work.

Permits

Permit and inspection steps are part of the schedule.

Insurance may cover resulting damage from a sudden leak but often does not cover the wear of the pipe itself. Some policies offer a service line endorsement, so ask your agent.

Service line work is regulated under 248 CMR, and the federal Lead and Copper Rule drives utility programs on lead service lines.
