Emergency toilet repair in Stoneham, MA is usually one of four things: a clog, a part inside the tank that has worn out, a leak at the base, or a toilet that has come loose from the floor. Each has a different fix, and most are done in a single visit.

This page helps you tell them apart, shows what you can safely try first, and explains when a plumber is the better call. If a toilet is overflowing right now, jump to the urgent section.

Toilet overflowing: what to do right now

An overflowing toilet puts waste water on the floor, and it can run through to the ceiling below. Stop the water first, then deal with the cause. Our line is open 24/7 if it does not clear.

Do these things in order.

  1. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop the water, or turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise.
  2. Do not flush again, and keep other fixtures off if the toilet is backing up from the line.
  3. Put towels around the base and move rugs, and wear gloves if you must handle the water.
  4. Use a flange plunger for a single clog, and give it a firm, steady series of pushes.
  5. If water rises again or other fixtures gurgle, call the 24/7 line, because the clog may be in the main.

Running, weak flush or phantom flushes

A toilet that runs, hisses or refills on its own is leaking water from the tank into the bowl. The flapper, the fill valve or the float is worn. A running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons a day, and it will show up as a higher water bill.

A simple test uses food coloring in the tank. If color shows in the bowl without a flush, the flapper is leaking. If the water level rises up to the overflow tube, the fill valve needs replacement.

Leaks at the base and loose toilets

Water at the foot of a toilet often means the wax ring beneath it has failed. The ring seals the toilet to the drain flange in the floor. A toilet that rocks can break that seal, and a slow leak can rot the subfloor over time.

The plumber removes the toilet, checks the flange for cracks and height, replaces the ring and sets the toilet back with new bolts. If the floor feels soft, they will tell you what they see before closing it up.

When the clog is more than the toilet

A single toilet that clogs once is almost always a local problem. A toilet that clogs often, or that makes other fixtures gurgle, points to the main drain or the vent. A bathtub that fills when the toilet is flushed is the classic sign.

In that case, a drain cleaning visit with a larger cable is the next step, and a camera shows what is in the line. Do not use chemical drain cleaners in a toilet, because they sit in the bowl and can splash on the person who plunges it.

Low-flow toilets and older fixtures

Federal rules since the 1990s set toilet flush volume at 1.6 gallons, and many newer models use 1.28 gallons or less. A toilet from before that era may use more water, and a replacement can pay back through lower bills.

Some older toilets in Stoneham homes with mid-century bathrooms are worth repairing. Others have cracked bowls or tanks, or enough wear that a new toilet is cheaper than another repair. We tell you which case you have.

What to try yourself first

  • Use a flange plunger, not a cup plunger, for toilets.
  • Try a toilet auger if the plunger does not work, and turn it gently to avoid scratching the bowl.
  • Check the shutoff valve and the supply line for drips.
  • Replace the flapper, which is an inexpensive part found at any hardware store.
  • Stop and call if you see water on the ceiling below, if the toilet is cracked, or if the floor feels soft.

What affects the price of toilet repair

Which part

A flapper or fill valve is a short repair. A wax ring reset takes longer, and a flange repair adds more.

Clog depth

A bowl clog clears quickly. A clog past the trap or in the main needs a larger tool.

Floor condition

A soft or damaged floor around the toilet may need repair before the toilet is reset.

New toilet

If the toilet is cracked or very worn, a replacement is the better value, and the price depends on the model.
