Plumber in Stoneham Center (Central Square), MA

Stoneham Center is the town's downtown, around Main Street, Central Street and the Town Common. Homes, shops and restaurants share the same few blocks, and plumbing questions here often involve a building and not just a fixture.

Main Street has been the town center since the Andover and Medford Turnpike opened in 1806. The Central Square Historic District, listed on the National Register in 1990, is roughly bounded by Main, Central, Church, Winter and Common Streets, and a state historical survey notes brick commercial blocks from the middle of the nineteenth century.

A 2015 regional planning report counted about 1,300 households in Stoneham Center, close to 15 percent of the town. It also lists newer housing, including a 47-unit condominium building and a 25-unit rental building near the square. That mix of old blocks, new buildings, shops and homes is what makes plumbing here different from a street of single-family houses.

Who is responsible for which pipe

In a building with more than one unit, a leak can start in one place and show up in another. Before you call, find out whether the problem is inside your unit or in a shared line. Shared risers, building drains and the main water shutoff are usually the owner's or the association's concern, and the fixtures and supply lines inside your unit may be yours.

Call the building manager or association first if water is coming through a ceiling or a wall. Then call us to look at the part that is yours. If you are not sure whose it is, describe it and we will tell you what we can see.

Plumbing for shops, restaurants and offices

Downtown storefronts and restaurants have needs a house does not, such as floor drains, kitchen lines and hot water for several sinks. See commercial plumbing for the services we handle, and ask about hydro jetting when a line keeps backing up after cable cleaning.

Food businesses often have rules about grease control. Ask Stoneham Public Works or the Building Division which rules apply to your space before you buy or change equipment.

Condo and apartment owners

  • Know where your unit's shutoff valve is, and where the building's main shutoff is.
  • Replace a water heater before it fails, since a tank that leaks in an upper unit damages the unit below.
  • Report a slow drain early, because a drain cleaning visit costs less than a backup into a neighbor's unit.
  • Tell us the building entrance, the floor and how we get in, so the visit goes smoothly.

Close to Nobility Hill
