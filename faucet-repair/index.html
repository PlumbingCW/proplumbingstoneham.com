Emergency faucet repair in Stoneham, MA covers the faucet that will not shut off, the leak that is dripping into a cabinet, and the outdoor spigot that split after a freeze. Most faucet problems come from worn internal parts, and a plumber can usually fix the faucet without replacing it.

Below you will find the common faults, how to tell which part has worn, and when it is better to replace the faucet entirely. If water is spraying or pooling, start with the urgent section.

Faucet leaking and will not stop

A faucet leak is only an emergency if it cannot be stopped or is damaging the cabinet or floor. A shutoff valve under the sink usually stops it. If the valve is stuck or leaks too, the main shutoff is the next step, and our 24/7 line can help.

Take these steps.

  1. Turn the small oval handle on the supply line under the sink clockwise to shut off the water.
  2. If the valve will not turn or drips, shut off the main water valve near the meter.
  3. Empty the cabinet under the sink and dry it, then place a bucket under any drip.
  4. Open the faucet to drain the pressure from the line.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from.

Why faucets drip and how each type is fixed

Inside every faucet is a part that stops the flow. In older compression faucets, it is a rubber washer pressed against a seat. In newer cartridge, ball and ceramic disc faucets, it is a sealed cartridge or disc set. Over time the washer hardens, the seat pits, or the O-rings wear out, and water gets past.

Low flow and weak pressure at one faucet

If a single faucet runs weakly while the others are fine, the aerator at the spout is the first place to look. Mineral and debris clog the small screen. Unscrew it, rinse it and put it back. If that does not help, the cartridge or a partly closed shutoff valve may be the issue.

If every faucet in the house is weak, the problem is bigger than a faucet, and the water service line or the pressure reducing valve is worth checking. Our water line repair page covers the supply side.

Outdoor faucets, freeze damage and winter

Outdoor spigots in New England take freeze damage when a hose is left attached. Water trapped in the stem freezes, expands and splits the pipe inside the wall. The leak often shows only when the spigot is turned on in spring, as water pouring into the wall.

Disconnect hoses before the first hard freeze and shut off the interior valve feeding the spigot where one exists. A frost-free hose bib with a long stem is an upgrade that reduces the risk. If a spigot has already split, see burst pipe repair for the next steps.

Repair or replace the faucet?

Repair is the right call when the faucet is in good shape and only a worn part has failed. Replacement is better when the finish is corroded, the body is cracked, parts are no longer made, or the faucet is a type that has repeated problems.

If you are replacing, bring the old model number or a photo. We install new faucets on request, and if you want a particular style, you can supply it, or we can recommend a reliable brand.

Things a homeowner can check

  • Identify the faucet type by looking at the handles and the way it moves.
  • Close the shutoff valves before opening the faucet body.
  • Take photos as you disassemble, so you know how the parts fit back together.
  • Bring the old cartridge to the hardware store so you get the exact match.
  • Stop and call if the shutoff valve does not close or if corrosion is severe.
