A tankless water heater in Stoneham, MA heats water as it flows instead of storing it, which means no standby losses and no running out after a long shower. The tradeoff is that the unit needs a larger gas line or a heavy electric circuit, a proper vent, and regular descaling to stay efficient.

This page covers whether tankless makes sense for your home, what the installation involves, and how service works. For a failed tank that needs to be swapped quickly, see water heater replacement.

Is tankless right for your home?

Tankless suits households that want endless hot water, have limited space, or are replacing a tank that has reached the end of its life. It is less compelling when the gas line is small and a new one is expensive, or when the house has very low hot water use.

The unit is sized by flow rate in gallons per minute and by the temperature rise it must deliver. Incoming water in New England is cold in winter, so the same unit that works easily in summer may struggle in January. We size for the coldest water.

What the installation needs

A gas tankless unit burns a lot of gas quickly, so the gas pipe that fed a small tank may be too small. The plumber checks the pipe size and the meter, and replaces a section if needed. The vent is usually sealed plastic that runs out through a wall, and the unit makes acidic condensation that has to drain to a floor drain or a neutralizer.

Electric tankless units draw very high current and often need a dedicated heavy circuit and a panel with room for it. We tell you before quoting if the electrical side is the limiting factor.

  • Gas line sized for the unit's maximum input
  • Sealed vent and combustion air pipes through an exterior wall
  • Condensate drain with a neutralizer where required
  • Isolation valves so the unit can be flushed
  • Pressure relief and a connection for a recirculation line if you want one

Descaling and maintenance

Even soft water leaves some scale over time, and the heat exchanger in a tankless unit is sensitive to it. Stoneham's MWRA water is soft, at about 1 grain per gallon, so scale builds slowly. A yearly flush with a descaling solution and a clean of the inlet filter keep it running well.

Skipping maintenance shows up as a drop in flow, a rise in temperature swings, or an error code. If you have a unit that is a few years old and has never been flushed, call us for a service visit.

Error codes and no hot water

Each brand uses its own codes, but the causes are familiar: a clogged inlet filter, a blocked vent, a gas supply issue, a flame sensor that needs cleaning, or scale. Tell the dispatcher the make, the model and the code on the display, and they can tell you whether it needs a plumber's visit.

If the unit has stopped and you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside. For other no-hot-water calls, our water heater repair team can diagnose a tankless unit as well as a tank.

Rebates and costs

Massachusetts utilities and the Mass Save program have offered incentives for efficient water heaters, though programs and amounts change. Check with your electric or gas provider before you buy, since eligibility can depend on the model and the fuel.

We can give you the model numbers and written details that most rebate forms ask for. The price of a tankless project is higher than a tank swap, and it pays back over time through lower fuel use and a longer lifespan.

What affects the price of tankless water heater

Fuel and unit size

Gas and electric units differ, as do models rated for two bathrooms and models rated for five.

Gas line or electrical upgrade

A larger gas pipe or a heavy circuit is often the largest extra cost.

Venting

A short run through an exterior wall costs less than a long run or a chimney conversion.

Condensate drain

A nearby floor drain is easy. A pump or a long drain line adds work.

Removal of the old tank

Haul-away of the old unit is part of the job.

Planned upgrades are generally not covered by insurance. Rebate programs are a separate source of savings, so ask your utility before the project starts.

Tankless installs follow 248 CMR and the manufacturer's installation manual, which sets gas input, vent material and clearances.
